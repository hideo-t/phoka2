FOR ISLAND COMMUNITIES / To island local governments

For island sustainability,
somewhere to liveas the answer.

In normal times, a base for relocation and tourism. In an emergency, shelter and temporary housing.
Housing where a single investment does two jobs.

The difficulties islands have taken for granted

Have you given up on that inconvenience,
assuming it is just the geography?

Housing and disaster-preparedness problems that arise precisely because you are away from the main island.
Let us put each of them into words first.

🚢 Schedules stretch while you wait for materials

Everything depends on ferries from the main island. Waiting for materials pushes the schedule back, bit by bit.

🌀 When logistics stop, the work stops

Rough seas mean no boats. Every typhoon halts work on site, and the completion date becomes impossible to predict.

🆘 In a disaster, you are isolated until help arrives

The few days before support arrives from the main island. There are not enough places on the island where people can take shelter.

🏠 You want to invite people, but there is nowhere to live

Even when you want to welcome new residents, housing ready to live in is scarce. Vacant homes exist, but often cannot be used as they are.

The core answer

Not building it,
but delivering and installing it.

Rather than building a home from scratch on site, we finish it at the factory, ship it to the island and set it in place.
That makes the schedule far less vulnerable to island weather and labor availability.

🏭

Built at the factory

Produced indoors, out of the weather, with quality kept under control.

➜
🚚

Shipped to the island

The finished home is transported, keeping on-site work to a minimum.

➜
🔧

Installed and put to use

Set it in place and living or operations can begin.
(We advise on the likely period from installation to first use individually, according to site conditions.)

So the completion date becomes predictable.
Far less affected by a shortage of island craftsmen or waiting for materials to arrive.

One investment that works twice

Useful in peacetime, protective in a crisis.

Normally it works for the community; when it matters, it protects lives.
One and the same unit carries both roles.

PEACETIME / Normal times

Creating living and activity

It operates as housing for new residents, or as a base for tourism and exchange. It welcomes people and generates both footfall and revenue for the community.

EMERGENCY / In a crisis

Protecting lives as a place of refuge

In a disaster it can be turned over to shelter or temporary housing. Until help arrives from the main island, it becomes a place on the island where people can take refuge.

Because it is in use day to day,
it never becomes a public asset that sits there unused.

To explain it to your council or superiors

Material that lets you say
“I can explain this.”

We have organized the talking points so you can use them directly in an internal approval process or a council session.

📈

It does not sit idle in normal times

Because the facility is in regular use, it is less likely to draw criticism for sitting unused, and easier to justify on cost-effectiveness.

🔄

It can be moved or redeployed

If demand or circumstances change, you can move it. Rather than a one-off investment, it keeps earning its keep over time.

🧭

It fits the wider policy context

It is easy to frame as part of relocation, settlement or community-building initiatives, which makes the purpose easier to explain.

* Final decisions on budget line items and project classification rest with each local government's own rules and practice. We help you prepare explanatory materials and organize the arguments. We do not offer definitive guidance on this point.

Frequently asked questions

What the people in charge
most often ask about.

QHow should we set the budget line item?＋
Rules and practice differ by local government, so we will not assert an answer. We will show you how such cases have been framed before and work through it with you on a consultative basis, on the understanding that the final decision on the line item rests with your authority.
QHow durable is it against typhoons and salt damage?＋
We plan to a specification that assumes Okinawa's climate. Specific specifications and figures will be explained individually in materials once confirmed.(Detailed specifications are still being confirmed, so we have refrained from stating figures on this page.)
QHow much land and what kind of foundation does installation need?＋
Installation conditions vary with the size of the unit and the local terrain. We will discuss the site area and foundation approach individually, based on your location.
QHow is conversion for disaster use treated under the rules?＋
Treatment under the rules varies with the situation and the timing. We will respond to your inquiry while working through the intended patterns of use.
